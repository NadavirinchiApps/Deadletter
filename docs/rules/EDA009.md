# EDA009 — Stream shard is read by too many shared-throughput consumers

**Condition:** Any sustained throughput near the shard's read limit.

## What it detects

Counts the shared-throughput readers polling one stream and compares them
against what its shards can serve.

A shard serves 2 MB/s of reads, shared across every consumer that polls it
directly. AWS documents two as the practical ceiling; a third reader takes
throughput from the other two rather than adding any. Every consumer's iterator
age then climbs together, and records can expire from the stream before the
slowest reader reaches them.

Consumers registered for enhanced fan-out have their own 2 MB/s and are not
counted against the shared budget.

## What the finding can carry

| Axis | Values this rule uses |
|---|---|
| `impact` | `STALL` |
| `confidence` | `CONFIRMED` |
| `basis` | `REQUIREMENT` |
| Can it BLOCK under `--policy default`? | Yes |

`impact` is what breaks, `confidence` is how well the condition is known, and
`basis` is whether the threshold is AWS's rule or AWS's advice. The verdict is
computed from those three by the policy you choose — the rule never sets it.

## What it rests on

- [Kinesis Data Streams quotas and limits](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/streams/latest/dev/service-sizes-and-limits.html)
- [Developing enhanced fan-out consumers](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/streams/latest/dev/enhanced-consumers.html)

## What it cannot know

Actual throughput. The limit is on aggregate read bandwidth, and a stream
whose readers are all idle will not hit it — but the shard count and the reader
count are both written in the template, and the ratio between them is what AWS
documents a limit on.

## Also reported by

Nothing measured. See [the benchmark](../benchmark.md) for how that was
established, and against which versions of which tools.

## Example

From `tests/fixtures/EDA009/violating.yaml`:

```text
[BLOCK] EDA009 - Stream shard is read by too many shared-throughput consumers
  ClickStream is polled by 4 shared-throughput readers (AnalyticsFunction#FromStream, ArchiveFunction#FromStream, FraudFunction#FromStream, PersonalisationFunction#FromStream), while its 1 shard(s) serve 2 MB/s across all of them. Required: at most 2 shared readers, or enhanced fan-out for the rest. Consequence: their GetRecords calls compete for the same read budget, every consumer's iterator age climbs together, and records expire from the stream before the slowest reader reaches them.
  basis: confirmed requirement | impact if it bites: stall
```

## Suppressing it

Suppressions live in the template, beside the resource they excuse. A reason is
mandatory, `expires` is honoured, and nothing is deleted — a suppressed finding
stays in the JSON report and is marked in SARIF.

```yaml
Resources:
  ClickStream:
    Metadata:
      deadletter:
        ignore:
          - rule: EDA009
            reason: Why this is acceptable here, and who decided.
            expires: 2027-01-31   # optional
```
